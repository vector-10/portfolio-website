summary:
  problem: >-
    Traditional credit scores completely ignore freelancers and gig workers
    because they don't have regular corporate payslips. While open banking apps
    can fetch their real account data to prove they are reliable, this data
    doesn't arrive all at once. It trickles in from different places at
    different times. If a user tries to apply while the system is still waiting
    on half of their information, the system breaks, crashes, or makes a credit
    decision based on an incomplete profile.
  did: >-
    I built Mono-Parser, a smart engine that securely gathers and organizes all
    these scattered financial pieces before a decision is made. I changed the
    workflow so the platform forces the main user account to be created first,
    locking everything in place. Then, I set up a background queue system that
    patiently collects the income data and statement history at its own pace. It
    retries automatically if there's a delay, and natively blocks duplicate
    requests so the system doesn't get overloaded or hit with extra fees.
  result: >-
    Lenders can now confidently look past traditional credit scores and
    instantly assess high-earning freelancers, contract developers, and gig
    workers who were previously invisible to the system. By assembling a
    multi-dimensional, fully auditable financial snapshot for every applicant,
    the engine gives loan managers the exact data points and stack metrics they
    need to confidently say "yes" or "no" to a loan. This opens up a massive,
    untouched segment of disciplined borrowers while keeping risk entirely
    predictable.
