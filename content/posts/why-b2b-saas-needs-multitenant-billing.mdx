## The pattern

Early on, a single flat-rate plan is enough, and billing logic stays simple. The moment a second enterprise customer wants usage-based pricing, or a discount schedule, or invoicing on their own terms, that simple billing logic starts accumulating special cases per customer.

## Why it gets expensive later

Without tenant isolation designed in from the start, billing logic ends up scattered with per-customer conditionals. Every new enterprise deal risks breaking an existing customer's billing, and auditing "what does customer X actually get charged" becomes a manual, error-prone exercise.

## The fix, done early

Multi-tenant billing with tenant-level plan configuration, enforced at the schema level rather than in scattered application logic. It costs more upfront than a flat-rate table, but it's a fixed cost paid once — not a growing one paid on every new enterprise deal.
